## Marnix van Berchum

### CV

After my graduation at Utrecht University in 2002 (MA; thesis on and editions of the motets of Jachet Berchem), my career has consisted of two parallel streams. Starting my first job at the Utrecht University Humanities Library, the non-musicological track led me -- via positions at SURF and DANS -– to my current job as Product Owner / Coordinator of the joint digital repository for digital collections and data at the Humanities Cluster of the KNAW (Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences). In parallel, I have continued my research in music of the sixteenth century. From 2006 onwards I became involved in the Computerized Mensural Music Editing project (CMME) and got acquainted with the (early) developments of MEI; in 2024 I contributed to the much needed conversion of CMME-XML to MEI. I have published, presented, and lectured on topics related to early music editing, data formats and digital musicology. One topic that has occupied my mind over the years is network science and its use in musicology. This culminated in March 2026 in a PhD on the application of the methods and tools of network science on the repertory of the polyphonic Te deum laudamus in the sixteenth century.

### Candidate Statement

I am honoured to be nominated as candidate to serve on the MEI board. I would love to actively contribute to the work I have been following for many years. If elected I hope my combined experience in the areas of research data, Open Access, Digital Humanities and musicology can contribute to the sustainable future of MEI as a technical and musicological standard, as well as a vibrant and multifaceted community. A topic I would like to focus on is the preservation of and access to MEI corpora for the larger (musicological) community. Furthermore I am interested in how MEI maintains the balance between being a generic, widely accepted standard and at the same time being able to support very specific use cases found in the richness of music history.

## Vera Grund

### CV

My professional journey in musicology began with a digital edition project, the Digital Mozart Edition, where I worked as a research assistant from 2007 to 2009. Through this work, I became familiar with and came to appreciate the digital research projects in Paderborn and attended the second Edirom Summer School. Following several other professional engagements – including my contribution to the digital catalogue of Gluck’s works with the ballet section – I became a member of the Virtuelle Forschungsverbund Edirom (ViFE) in 2017.
After nearly six years at the Department of Musicology in Detmold, I took up my current position as Head of the Department for Music History  at the German Historical Institute (DHI) in Rome in 2024. Since then, I have maintained close ties with ViFE through teaching and collaborative research projects. Among other things, one part of the multi-module, interdisciplinary digital edition project “Dance/Music digital” moved with me to the DHI in Rome, while the other remained at the Department of Musicology in Detmold under the direction of Andreas Münzmay.

### Candidate Statement

I was delighted to receive the nomination to stand for election to the MEI Board and would like to express my gratitude for it. Having followed the work of the MEI community with great interest for many years, I would welcome the opportunity to contribute more actively to its future development as a member of the Board.

It is a particular pleasure and a reflection of our close ties with the MEI community that we will be hosting the 2027 Music Encoding Conference at the Department for Music History in Rome. As members of the MEC’s local organising committee, we are very much looking forward to the conference and hope to welcome many of you to Rome.
My particular research interest in digital scholarly editing lies at the intersection of music and dance, extending to other dimensions of performance, such as staging and spatial sound. I am especially interested in exploring how MEI can support interdisciplinary research and the representation of the complex relationships between these different dimensions of performance.

As a member of the MEI Board, I would be delighted to contribute this expertise, together with my experience in organising and coordinating interdisciplinary digital research projects and serving on academic and research policy boards, committees and organisations. I would welcome the opportunity to support the continued development of MEI and its open and collaborative research community.

## Stefan Münnich 

### CV

After studying musicology and communication science at the Technische Universität Berlin, where I graduated in 2011 with a MA thesis on cantional settings in Heinrich Schütz’s Becker-Psalter, I worked for the edition project Felix Mendelssohn Bartholdy. Sämtliche Briefe at the University of Leipzig between 2012 and 2015 (co-editor of vols. 9 & 12). Since October 2015 I have been a research associate of the Anton Webern Gesamtausgabe at the University of Basel, where I finished my PhD thesis on music notation and code by the end of 2019.

### Candidate Statement

It is a great honour to be nominated again for the MEI Board. I have always found the MEI community to be a welcoming, open-minded and inclusive community, characterized by the rich experience, expertise and diversity of the people involved in it. As a member of the Board and Technical Team for the past 6 years, I have been delighted to contribute to this living vision and to support more people in their diverse needs, interest and commitment within the MEI community, and I would be happy to continue to advocate for this.

## Silke Reich

### CV

I studied musicology, philosophy and history at Goethe University Frankfurt, completing my degree in 2017 with a master's thesis on _Georg Philipp Telemanns mehrsprachiges Musikdrama ›Orpheus‹ als Experiment der Hamburger Oper_. From 2017 to 2020, I pursued a second degree in _Digitalen Methodik in den Geistes- und Kulturwissenschaften_ (Digital Humanities) at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz. My master's thesis examined the semantic modelling of work catalogues, using the _Gluck Catalogue of Works_ as a case study.

During this period, I also qualified as a Linux system administrator and worked in this role at the HeBIS Network Centre from 2018 to 2020. Since April 2021, I have been a research associate at the _Erich Wolfgang Korngold Werkausgabe_ and have been pursuing a PhD on modelling audiovisual media in digital thematic catalogues, using the _Korngold Catalogue of Works and Sources_ as a case study.

### Candidate Statement
As a research software engineer at the Erich Wolfgang Korngold Werkausgabe, I work at the intersection of musicological research, editorial practice and technical implementation. Alongside the thematic catalogue, I contribute to the digital edition of Korngold's film music, helping to develop an emerging area of digital scholarly editing. My PhD research examines thematic catalogues and their data models, bringing together cultural analysis and musicological expertise, and explores how film music can be represented using MEI. My engagement with data modelling and MEI dates back to my university studies, including my master's dissertation.

My involvement with the MEI Metadata Interest Group and the MerMEId community has shown me the value of exchange across communities. In our project, I connect digital research with editorial practice, and have considerable experience facilitating communication between musicology and the digital humanities. I would bring this experience to the Board, helping connect researchers, editors, developers and cultural heritage institutions, and encouraging dialogue across disciplinary and community boundaries.

I would be pleased to contribute to a useful, sustainable and inclusive MEI community.
