Statement on the use of generative AI in submissions for MEC 2027

We recognize that authors of academic works use a variety of tools in the research on which they report, and to prepare the report itself, from simple ones to very sophisticated ones. We note that tools may generate useful and helpful results, but also errors or misleading claims. Knowing which tools were used is relevant to evaluating and interpreting academic works.

In view of this, we:

  1. require authors to report in their work any significant use of software tools, such as instruments and software that go beyond word processors or typesetters; we now include in particular text-to-text generative AI among those that should be reported.
  2. remind all colleagues that by signing their name as an author of a contribution, they each individually take full responsibility for all its contents, irrespective of how the contents were generated. If use of generative AI results in inappropriate language, plagiarized content, errors, mistakes, incorrect references, or misleading content, and that output is included in academic works, it is the responsibility of the author(s). This may affect acceptance of your submission.
  3. do not accept generative AI language tools as an author; instead authors should refer to (1).

Statement on the use of AI for reviews

At MEC 2027, we promote an AI-free review process. In short, any use of AI tools and LLMs in review drafting and polishing is prohibited. Any non-adherence to this policy might lead to disqualification from being a reviewer for future MEC conferences.

Compared to the AI usage policies for authors, the AI usage policy for reviewers is more strict to prevent any possible leakage of ideas or code of the submissions. Reviewers must keep everything relating to the review process confidential. Reviewers cannot share any materials of the submissions (e.g., paper, code, other supplementary materials) with LLMs. It is not allowed to use LLMs to fix grammatical issues or to smooth the writing of your reviews either. Language-related mistakes are acceptable and the content of the review is what counts, not its smooth delivery - it is better that we have typos and grammar mistakes but are 100% confident that the assigned reviewer wrote the review.

Reviewers are responsible for the quality and accuracy of their submitted review regardless of any tools, resources, or other help they used to construct the final review.

This statement is an adapted version of the arXiv policy for authors’ use of generative AI language tools and ISMIR's policy for reviewers. We reserve the right to amend this statement as discussions continue and evolve.
